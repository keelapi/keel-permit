# Permit-to-X Admission Test v1

A customer-visible specific Permit title is admitted only when every applicable
gate passes. Failure at any gate retains an honest generic or unclassified
fallback.

1. **Stable meaning** — the action has one stable business meaning in the
   supported context.
2. **Trusted identity** — server-owned provenance establishes a canonical
   semantic identity.
3. **Governed boundary** — Keel governs the relevant surface before dispatch.
4. **Typed scope** — consequential constraints are typed and fail closed.
5. **Exact binding** — the signed request binding covers every consequential
   field.
6. **Evidence** — runtime evidence supports the promised facts and states its
   negative space.
7. **Verifier parity** — every new external claim is adjudicated by the released
   public verifier.
8. **Honest presentation** — UI, terminal report, and documentation distinguish
   authorization from later outcomes.
9. **Relationship semantics** — standalone and linked behavior is explicit and
   safe.
10. **Environment reality** — connector, entitlement, and rollout prerequisites
    are real in the target environment.
11. **Customer value** — the title communicates materially more than generic
    `AI Permit` plus the exact action description.
12. **Implication audit** — the title does not imply consent, identity, legality,
    completion, truth, quality, safety, settlement, or comprehensive recording
    that Keel does not establish.

The initial admitted exact actions are payment execution and server-owned text
generation. Realtime is generic-qualified as `AI Permit — Realtime session`;
this contract does not admit Permit-to-Speak, Permit-to-Talk,
Permit-to-Converse, or Permit-to-Call.

An already trusted, governed, typed, and evidenced action passes the foundation
extensibility test only when adding its presentation requires registry rows,
generated artifacts, fixtures, generic UI data, and documentation—without a
database migration, endpoint family, Permit lifecycle change, signing-profile
redesign, Policy-engine change, verifier claim/verdict change, or action-specific
dashboard component.
